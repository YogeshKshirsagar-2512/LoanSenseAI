Dataset Limitations

Critical data characteristics that impact predictive reliability, fairness governance, and real-world banking deployment.

The target variable is heavily skewed toward rejection (68.6% vs 31.4% approved). Naive models risk predicting the majority class exclusively while yielding deceptively high raw accuracy.

Impact

  • Accuracy paradox: 68.6% baseline accuracy with zero predictive skill
  • Reduced sensitivity (recall) on creditworthy minority applicants

Mitigation

Class Weight Balancing F1-Score Optimization Stratified K-Fold

Exactly 50 values (5.0%) are missing across every column, including the target label. Real-world financial records have non-uniform missingness reflecting operational gaps.

Impact

  • 50 rows with null targets discarded prior to model training
  • Synthetic imputation rules directly govern feature distributions

Mitigation

Median/Mode Imputer Target-Null Row Exclusion

1,000 records (950 usable post-cleaning) limits the model's capacity to represent rare financial edge cases and increases cross-validation variance.

Impact

  • Higher generalization error on non-linear estimators like KNN
  • Test partition limited to 200 holdout applications

Mitigation

Cross-Validation L2 Regularization

The dataset contains 571 males (60%) vs 379 females (40%). In actual production lending, protected attributes such as gender are governed under ECOA/Fair Lending legislation.

Impact

  • Potential for automated bias amplification in production underwriting
  • Demographic disparity in model acceptance decisions

Mitigation

Disparate Impact Audit Fair Lending Note

Most categorical features show virtually identical acceptance rates across categories (e.g. Property Area is ~30% across Urban, Semiurban, and Rural).

Impact

  • High dimensionality without corresponding increase in signal
  • Model reliance is concentrated heavily on Credit Score & DTI

Mitigation

Enhanced Quadratic Features StandardScaler Normalization

Identical 5.0% missing rates, perfectly uniform loan purpose counts (178–202 per category), and lack of data collisions confirm simulated dataset construction.

Impact

  • Ideal for ML architecture & engineering workflows, not live bank underwriting
  • Macroeconomic covariances are not represented

Mitigation

Educational Context Transparent Disclaimers

No timestamps or application dates exist. Credit cycles, interest rate changes, and inflation shifts cannot be captured by static feature snapshots.

Impact

  • Inability to model concept drift over economic cycles

Mitigation

Cross-sectional Modeling

Education and gender are binary; property area is reduced to 3 classifications, obscuring micro-geographic risk variations.

Impact

  • Loss of granular credit risk variance across regional jurisdictions

Mitigation

Feature Augmentation

Key Empirical Insights

Key data-driven findings revealing the primary mathematical drivers behind loan underwriting decisions.

INSIGHT 01 High Separability

Credit Score is the Primary Determinant

Approved applicants average 726.9 vs 651.6 for rejected cases — a significant 75.3-point spread. The minimum approved score is 651, defining a clear underwriting threshold.

Threshold Spread +75.3 points
INSIGHT 02 Low Variance

Income Confers Minor Marginal Value

The mean income delta between approved (₹11,797) and rejected (₹10,406) cohorts is only ₹1,391. Both cohorts overlap across the entire ₹2,000 to ₹20,000 spectrum.

Mean Income Delta ₹1,391 only
INSIGHT 03 Anomaly

Contract Workers Lead Approvals

Counter-intuitively, contract workers exhibit the highest approval rate at 39.7%, exceeding salaried corporate professionals at 29.2% — an empirical artifact of the training data.

Contract Rate 39.7% Acceptance
INSIGHT 04 Neutral Factor

Location Has Negligible Predictive Influence

Acceptance probabilities are virtually identical across geographic sectors: Urban (31.8%), Rural (30.2%), and Semiurban (30.0%) with a maximum divergence of only 1.8%.

Max Spread 1.8% Spread
